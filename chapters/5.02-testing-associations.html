5.2 Testing Associations

Testing correlation coefficients for significance.

You arrive here from Hypothesis Testing, so you already know how hypothesis testing works, and you can compute a correlation to describe a relationship. Here you’ll test whether a correlation in your sample is statistically different from zero with cor.test(). A correlation in a sample can arise by chance, so testing it tells you how much confidence you can place in the relationship — and it prepares you for the linear model, since cor.test() turns out to be a t-test in disguise.

5.2.2 Testing a correlation with cor.test()

In empirical research, we do not merely calculate the sample correlation \(r\); we also test whether the correlation is statistically significantly different from zero in the population (\(H_0: \rho = 0\)).

We can perform this test using cor.test():

cor.test(d$x, d$y)

    Pearson's product-moment correlation

data:  d$x and d$y
t = 3.5762, df = 3, p-value = 0.03739
alternative hypothesis: true correlation is not equal to 0
95 percent confidence interval:
 0.08610194 0.99343752
sample estimates:
cor 
0.9

Interpreting the Output

  1. \(t\)-statistic and degrees of freedom: \(t(3) = 3.55\), where \(df = n - 2 = 5 - 2 = 3\).
  2. \(p\)-value: \(p = 0.038\), which is below the threshold of \(\alpha = .05\). We reject the null hypothesis and conclude that \(x\) and \(y\) are significantly positively correlated.
  3. 95% Confidence Interval: \([0.11, 0.99]\). Since the interval does not include \(0\), it further confirms statistical significance.

APA-Style Reporting

“A Pearson correlation revealed a strong, positive, and statistically significant relationship between the two variables, \(r(3) = .90\), \(p = .038\), 95% CI \([0.11, 0.99]\).”

The test behind cor.test()

Under the hood, cor.test() performs a t-test on the correlation coefficient: it computes \(t = r \sqrt{n - 2} / \sqrt{1 - r^2}\) and compares it to a t-distribution with \(n - 2\) degrees of freedom. So testing a correlation uses the same logic as the t-tests you saw in Comparing two Groups.
